The Nomad's Co-Working Arsenal: Essential Gear and Apps for Shared Workspaces

2026-07-14 · Remote Work · Tags: The, digitalnomadtech, remote work

As a digital nomad, your office is wherever you happen to be—but that doesn't mean you have to settle for a cramped café table or a noisy hostel lobby. Co-working spaces have become the lifeblood of the remote work lifestyle, offering reliable Wi-Fi, ergonomic chairs, and a community of like-minded professionals. Yet, walking into a co-working space without the right tools is like bringing a knife to a gunfight. To truly thrive in these shared environments, you need a carefully curated arsenal of portable devices, travel tech gear, and productivity apps that transform any desk into a high-performance command center. In this guide, I’ll share my personal toolkit, review some of the best co-working spaces I’ve worked from across the globe, and offer practical tips to help you build your own co-working arsenal.

The Rise of Co-Working Spaces for Nomads

Co-working spaces have exploded in popularity over the past decade, and for good reason. They offer a professional environment without the commitment of a long-term lease, and they provide the social interaction that remote work often lacks. For digital nomads, these spaces are more than just a place to plug in a laptop—they’re a hub for networking, collaboration, and even friendship. But not all co-working spaces are created equal. Some are buzzing with energy and equipped with the latest tech, while others are little more than a room with a few tables and spotty Wi-Fi. Knowing what to look for—and what to bring—can make the difference between a productive day and a frustrating one.

When I first started my nomadic journey, I made the mistake of assuming that any co-working space would have everything I needed. I quickly learned that even the best spaces have limitations. For instance, many provide monitors and keyboards, but they’re often generic and not tailored to your workflow. Others have excellent Wi-Fi but lack sufficient power outlets. That’s why building a personal arsenal is essential. It ensures you’re never at the mercy of the space’s shortcomings, and it allows you to work comfortably from anywhere—from a sleek WeWork in Berlin to a beachside Selina in Mexico.

Essential Portable Devices for Co-Working

Your laptop is the heart of your setup, but it’s not the only device you need. To maximize productivity in a co-working space, consider adding a few portable companions. A lightweight, high-resolution laptop is a given, but I also recommend carrying a compact external monitor. Models like the ASUS ZenScreen or the Lepow Z1 are USB-C powered and weigh under two pounds, making them easy to slip into a backpack. When you’re working on complex spreadsheets or editing videos, a second screen is a game-changer. It allows you to keep your email open on one side while you code or write on the other, reducing the need to constantly switch tabs.

Another essential is a reliable wireless keyboard and mouse. While many co-working spaces provide them, they’re often worn out or not to your liking. A low-profile mechanical keyboard like the Keychron K3 or a compact Logitech MX Keys Mini can dramatically improve your typing comfort and speed. Pair these with a precision mouse like the Logitech MX Master 3, and you’ll have a setup that rivals any office. Don’t forget a high-quality webcam and microphone if you’re doing video calls—the built-in ones on most laptops are mediocre. A small clip-on webcam like the Razer Kiyo or a USB mic like the Blue Snowball can make you look and sound professional, even in a bustling co-working space.

Travel Tech Gear That Enhances Co-Working

Beyond your core devices, the right travel tech gear can elevate your co-working experience. First and foremost is power. Co-working spaces often have limited outlets, and they’re not always conveniently located. A high-capacity power bank (at least 20,000mAh) is a lifesaver, but even better is a compact GaN charger with multiple ports. The Anker 735 or the Satechi 165W charger can juice up your laptop, phone, and earbuds simultaneously, so you’re never scrambling for a free socket. I also carry a universal travel adapter with surge protection—essential when you’re hopping between countries with different plug types.

Noise-cancelling headphones are non-negotiable in a shared workspace. The constant chatter, keyboard clatter, and phone conversations can destroy your focus. I’ve tested many, and the Sony WH-1000XM5 or the Bose QuietComfort 45 are the gold standard. They not only block out noise but also have excellent microphones for calls. If you prefer earbuds, the Apple AirPods Pro 2 or the Sony WF-1000XM5 offer similar performance in a smaller package. Another piece of gear I never leave home without is a portable Wi-Fi hotspot. Even the best co-working spaces can have internet outages, and having a backup connection via a 4G/5G hotspot ensures you’re never offline. The GlocalMe or Netgear Nighthawk are reliable options with global coverage.

Productivity Apps for Co-Working Environments

Your physical gear is only half the equation. The right software can streamline your workflow and help you stay organized, no matter where you’re working. For project management, I swear by Trello or Asana—both have free tiers and are perfect for tracking tasks and deadlines. When collaborating with a remote team, Slack or Microsoft Teams are essential for communication, but I also use Zoom or Google Meet for video calls. To manage my time effectively, I use Toggl Track or RescueTime, which help me understand how I’m spending my hours and identify distractions.

One app that has transformed my co-working experience is Focus@Will, which plays background music scientifically designed to enhance concentration. It’s perfect for drowning out ambient noise without the distraction of lyrics. For note-taking, Notion is my go-to—it’s a versatile tool that combines notes, databases, and wikis in one place. I also use Evernote for quick captures and scanning documents. Finally, don’t underestimate the power of a good password manager like 1Password or Bitwarden. When you’re using public Wi-Fi in co-working spaces, security is paramount, and a password manager ensures you’re not reusing weak passwords. Pair it with a VPN like NordVPN or ExpressVPN to encrypt your connection and protect your data from prying eyes.

Co-Working Space Reviews: From WeWork to Selina

Over the past three years, I’ve worked from dozens of co-working spaces across four continents. Here are a few that stand out, along with the tech considerations that made them great—or not so great.

WeWork (Global) – WeWork is the gold standard for consistency. Their spaces are always clean, well-designed, and equipped with fast Wi-Fi. I’ve used WeWork locations in Lisbon, Bangkok, and New York, and the experience is remarkably uniform. The desks are spacious, and there are plenty of power outlets. However, the noise level can be high in common areas, so my noise-cancelling headphones were essential. WeWork also offers phone booths for private calls, which is a huge plus. The main downside is the price—it’s on the higher end, but if you’re on a budget, their hot desk plans are still reasonable.

Selina (Latin America, Europe, and beyond) – Selina is a favorite among nomads because it combines co-working with accommodation. Their co-working spaces are often open-air, with a bohemian vibe that’s perfect for creative work. I worked from Selina in Medellín and Tulum, and the Wi-Fi was surprisingly reliable. However, the seating is less ergonomic—think hammocks and bean bags—so I always brought my own laptop stand and external keyboard. Selina’s community events are fantastic for networking, but if you need absolute quiet, you might struggle. Their day passes are affordable, and they offer discounts for guests.

Outsite (Various locations) – Outsite is a co-working and coliving brand that focuses on digital nomads. I stayed at their location in San Diego, and it was one of the best setups I’ve experienced. The dedicated co-working space is separate from the living quarters, with standing desks, ergonomic chairs, and high-speed fiber internet. They also have a well-equipped meeting room with a large monitor and video conferencing gear. The only downside is that Outsite locations are limited, but if you find one, it’s worth the investment. Their spaces are designed with remote workers in mind, so you’ll rarely need to bring extra gear beyond your laptop.

Tips for Maximizing Your Co-Working Experience

Even with the perfect arsenal, your co-working experience can be enhanced by a few simple habits. First, always arrive early to secure a good spot—preferably near a window for natural light and away from high-traffic areas. Second, establish a routine: set specific work hours and stick to them, just as you would in a traditional office. This helps you stay focused and also signals to others that you’re not available for chit-chat during deep work sessions. Third, take advantage of the community. Co-working spaces are networking goldmines. Introduce yourself, attend events, and collaborate on projects. You never know when a fellow nomad might become a client or a friend.

Another tip is to test the Wi-Fi speed before you commit to a day pass. Most spaces will let you do a quick speed test, and I always check for upload speeds, which are crucial for video calls. If the connection is poor, don’t hesitate to move to another space. Finally, keep your gear organized. A good backpack with dedicated compartments for your laptop, charger, and accessories will save you time and prevent damage. I use the Nomatic Travel Pack, which has a dedicated laptop sleeve and a built-in cable organizer. It’s a bit pricey, but it’s an investment that pays off in convenience.

Conclusion

Building your co-working arsenal is about more than just buying the latest gadgets—it’s about creating a portable office that adapts to any environment. By investing in a few key portable devices, travel tech gear, and productivity apps, you can turn any co-working space into a productive haven. Remember, the goal is not to carry everything you own, but to carry the right things that make you efficient and comfortable. Whether you’re working from a bustling WeWork in a metropolis or a serene Selina by the beach, your arsenal ensures you’re always ready to do your best work. So, pack your gear, download your apps, and head to your nearest co-working space with confidence. The world is your office, and with the right tools, you’ll conquer it one desk at a time.
